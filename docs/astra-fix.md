**2026-09-15 original follow-up analysis.** The following source audit describes v2 and motivated the later changes. The v4 review above supersedes its implementation-status statements; the capacity calculation and proposed GPU validation still apply.

**Why v2 protects A but barely advances B.** The relevant code is `_Glm53MixedPrefill` in the [scheduler overlay](../overlay/patch_scheduler_decode_floor.py). V2 accounting did not distinguish solo prefill from prefill competing with a decoder:

| V2 source path | V2 behavior | Consequence for the next revision |
|---|---|---|
| `note_scheduled()` → `observe_output()` | A positive scheduled prefill enters `step_prefill_ids`; `mixed = bool(served)` at completion, even if the step had no decoder. | A's solo prefill is charged as mixed work. Capture the actual batch composition when scheduling. |
| `_recent_share()` | Sums those durations over an 8 s window, retaining an entire sample until its completion timestamp ages out. | Recent solo chunks inflate `pre_s`; window expiry can abruptly change admission. Separate cost history from the contention budget. |
| `observe_output()` | Uses time between successive output callbacks, not measured GPU execution duration. The first sample has zero duration; later samples can include idle time or async output handling. | `pre_s` / `dec_s` are wall-time proxies, not an exact GPU-time partition. Fix attribution before tuning against them. |
| `last_prefill_turn_mono` / `_flush_prior_schedule()` | Any served prefill updates the global turn timestamp; any queued prefill IDs increment `inflight_mixed`. | Solo history affects the first mixed opportunity. An outstanding solo step still occupies the engine, but its completed cost must not become historical contention debt. |
| `cap_for()` / `_select()` | Fixed `chunk` tokens per selected request; at most `max_chunks` distinct requests. | There is no adaptive chunk-cost estimate or target prefill rate. Increasing `MAX_CHUNKS` cannot give a single B several chunks in one turn. |

`begin_step()` allows a turn when **any** of these holds: no previous prefill turn, recent share below target, time since the last completed prefill turn at least `interval_s`, or the oldest request's unserved age at least `interval_s`. The async-in-flight check runs before all four. Thus `2000 ms` is an escape from share deferral, **not a fixed minimum spacing**: turns can happen more often when share permits. Conversely, it is not a hard bound on completed service: an in-flight step, alignment, sequence slots, or KV capacity can delay progress. The observed approximately 256/2 s pattern is the fallback under share pressure, not an inherent rate cap of `fair`.

Excluding A's solo prefill should remove unnecessary share deferrals and permit an earlier first chunk. It does not eliminate B's remaining computation. Nor does `defer=share` prove B made zero progress: the retained JSON contains TTFT and stream summaries, not a per-step prefill trace. The approximately 22 s obtained by subtracting A's decode window from B's TTFT is a useful symptom, not a measured decomposition of queueing and GPU service.

**Check whether the requested TTFT fits the available service.** The cold receipt has 29,112 prompt tokens / 22.40 s for C1 and 30,618 / 23.43 s for A before overlap, about **1.3k prompt tokens/s** in both cases. These are end-to-end cold TTFT measurements for comparable A prompts, not a standalone B kernel benchmark. They are the best current scale estimate and should be replaced with a matched solo-B baseline in validation.

For a rough time-allocation model, let `P` be B's uncached tokens, `R` its prefill rate during service, `f` the fraction of engine time available to prefill, and `W` the remaining overlap window. Then `progress ≈ f × R × W` and, while a decoder remains continuously present, `TTFT ≈ P / (f × R)`, plus other delays. This assumes efficient chunks and ignores extra mixed-path overhead. Mixed turns also produce some A tokens, so `f` is not exactly A's throughput loss, and v2's callback-derived share is not an exact value of `f`.

| Illustrative service to one 30k cold B | Progress in 14 s | Time to prefill 30k if this allocation persists |
|---|---:|---:|
| 256 tokens every 2 s, ignoring chunk execution time | ~1.8k (~6%) | ~234 s |
| 20% of time at 1.3k tokens/s during service | ~3.6k (~12%) | ~115 s |
| 50% of time at that rate | ~9.1k (~30%) | ~46 s |
| Full engine time at that rate | ~18.2k (~61%) | ~23 s |

These are planning estimates, not new results. When A finishes, fair's mixed cap is lifted and B accelerates; that is why the observed 36 s need not resemble the continuous-decode estimates. At a 20% time allocation, completing 30k in 14 s would require **~10.7k tokens/s during prefill service**, over 8× the current cold rate estimate. Even full engine time at the measured rate does not fit 14 s. A scheduler-only change therefore cannot be promised to meet that target while preserving A's ~56–61 tok/s. Enlarging chunks might improve efficiency, but the required improvement must be measured.

**Original scheduler design requirements, in priority order.**

1. **Charge only work that competes with decode.** Record immutable metadata for each submitted step: step ID, final scheduled prefill token counts after all caps/allocation, eligible decode rows, and whether runnable prefill demand existed. Classify its completion from that record, including async/speculative and cached-prefix transitions. Contention means prefill delays an eligible decoder: a deliberate prefill-only turn still incurs debt even without a decode row in the final batch. Keep solo-prefill samples for cost estimation, but exclude them from the budget that protects concurrent decode. When transitioning from solo prefill to decode, begin with a bounded allowance for one conservative probe chunk; A's earlier prefill must not consume it. Keep shared debt across newcomer arrivals/cancellations while decode contention continues, so queue churn cannot repeatedly obtain free bursts. Retire outstanding work before resetting an epoch and reset timing across engine idle gaps.

2. **Replace the stale share threshold with bounded service credit.** Accrue credit at the configured share of accounted engine service, reserve the estimated cost before dispatch, and reconcile against completion. Conservatively charging the whole mixed-step duration is a safe starting model; subtracting a matched decode-only cost can be explored only after validating actual A throughput and delivery gaps. At share 0.20, a mixed turn costing `c` seconds requires about `4c` seconds of decode-only service per turn in steady state under the conservative model. Bound saved credit and borrowing so a quiet period cannot authorize a burst. Keep one prefill-bearing step in flight until costs are reliable. Pair records with the corresponding output, explicitly handling empty schedules, aborts, and preemptions instead of assuming every `schedule()` produces a callback. Use passive worker timings where available; otherwise label the corrected host timing as an estimate and validate it with a later trace, without adding a per-step GPU synchronization.

3. **Choose chunk size from cost and remaining work.** Estimate mixed-step duration and delivered prefill tokens for the actual B position, A history, decode count, speculative shape, and graph mode. Start from 256; select a chunk with good tokens-per-service-time that fits both the available credit and the allowed A delivery gap. A future bounded ladder of 256 → 512 → 1024 can establish whether larger chunks amortize overhead enough to help; shrink or defer when a shape exceeds the latency budget. Preserve `_glm53_align_prefill_limit`, mandatory hybrid cache boundaries, and resumed-state correctness, and record the actual post-alignment count. Keep the normal solo-prefill limit when no decoder competes. Track each B's remaining uncached work and achieved service rate so the policy reports an estimated completion time, rather than treating one admitted chunk as success.

4. **Make aging and decode protection compatible.** Give the oldest eligible prefill a bounded probe promptly, then age requests by their last completed positive prefill service. A deadline override may borrow at most a bounded chunk and must repay that debt; age cannot authorize an arbitrary large batch. A hard share ceiling and a hard 2 s service interval can conflict when chunks are expensive or several newcomers compete. Preserve the agreed decode floor when bounded borrowing cannot resolve the conflict, and log the missed prefill target; meeting both then requires cheaper chunks or more capacity. Define aggregate and per-request service separately; one chunk per global turn does not give each of three newcomers service every 2 s. Reserve eligible decodes' token/input/draft capacity explicitly before allocating prefill, including when a partial prefill precedes them in `running`. The v2 overlay inherited running-list order and added no such reservation pass; the earlier “decode reserved first” claim was too broad. Release tentative grants when alignment or allocation prevents execution, and select another eligible prefill where possible.

5. **Enforce a joint A/B target and expose infeasibility.** Use matched A-only throughput and actual overlap delivery gaps to control prefill admission. Treat the observed ~88–91% retention of solo decode throughput as the regression reference; any larger permitted loss needs an explicit target. Measure B's progress at fixed times after arrival and its TTFT against a matched solo-B run. If the deadline needs more service than the allowed A slowdown can supply, report that conflict instead of silently increasing prefill share. Never obtain a “B first token during A” pass merely by stretching A's decode until B finishes. A non-preemptible mixed step that already exceeds the gap target requires backend improvements or a different service allocation.

**The admission bug is explicit in the overlay.** This subsection describes the recipe / v1 helper. v2 still implements the same `skip` predicate when `CHUNK=skip`; `fair` replaces it. In `overlay/patch_scheduler_decode_floor.py`, `_glm53_mixed_prefill_policy()` scans `self.running`. If any other request has `num_computed_tokens >= num_prompt_tokens`, it returns zero for `skip`. The waiting-request insertion then executes:

```python
if mixed_cap <= 0:
    request_queue.pop_request()
    step_skipped_waiting.prepend_request(request)
    continue
```

The request is returned to the skipped waiting queue and encounters the same decision next time. There is no age limit, service credit, or maximum deferral count. A 2k prompt and a 30k prompt meet the same predicate. For a partially prefilling request already in `running`, the other insertion caps `num_new_tokens` to zero; the scheduler skips that request for the step. This also makes a running-count metric insufficient to prove progress. **Why `0` transfers the delay to A.** `0` makes the helper return `None`: the extra isolation policy is disabled. The scheduler allocates tokens to running requests first and then waiting requests. In this experiment A is already running when B arrives, so A can receive its small decode allocation and B can consume much of the remaining step budget. Once admitted, B continues chunked prefill in the running loop.

The two GB10s are tensor-parallel ranks of one engine. They execute that batch together; they are not independent workers for A and B. A’s next output depends on completing the shared model step. Reserving A a few token positions does not let it execute many additional decode iterations while the same step processes thousands of B’s tokens. DFlash k=7 adds verification work of up to eight positions per decoding request; adaptive-k changes that work, and accepted output tokens are not the same as scheduled positions. The scheduler also reserves drafting input slots, so the prefill allowance is not exactly `7168 - 8`.

The global threshold is applied to `num_new_tokens` in both running and waiting paths. It caps a request’s scheduled chunk, including solo prefill; it is neither a millisecond budget nor an aggregate prefill budget across all requests. At C4, several prefills can each receive their cap until the engine’s other budgets run out. Leaving token capacity unused is not the same as inserting additional decode iterations.

The backend makes this tradeoff more severe than a token-count model suggests. The recipe routes SM120 sparse-MLA prefill through its supported sparse path. The pinned `FlashInferMLASparseMetadataBuilder` declares `UNIFORM_BATCH` graph support; adding a long prefill does not preserve the uniform decode FULL-graph path. Mixed execution can still use applicable piecewise graphs. The overlay’s original rationale also records expensive mixed execution at a 128-token cap and 80k history. Sparse-indexer work, graph-path changes, MoE work, and TP synchronization are plausible contributors; **the supplied data does not identify their individual costs**. Do not label this an ABLIT, acceptance-rate, or NCCL defect without a trace. [Recipe backend adaptation](https://github.com/MiaAI-Lab/GLM-5.3-Flash-EXL3-2x-DGX-Sparks/blob/f906ee990596486e10ddbe381efa6f0e496f77e3/Dockerfile#L1), [adaptive-k implementation](../overlay/patch_adaptive_k.py).

Upstream also documents that smaller prefill batches generally improve inter-token latency at a cost to prefill performance. That supports tuning the tradeoff; it does not promise latency isolation on this backend. [vLLM chunked-prefill tuning](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/configuration/optimization/#performance-tuning-with-chunked-prefill).

**A second code hazard affects proposed small-cap fixes.** The pinned scheduler’s `_mamba_block_aligned_split()` runs after the mixed cap. In hybrid `mamba_cache_mode="align"`, its decision to allow sub-block progress uses:

```python
max_prefill_tokens = self.max_num_scheduled_tokens
if long_prefill_threshold > 0:
    max_prefill_tokens = min(max_prefill_tokens, long_prefill_threshold)
aligned_end = end // block_size * block_size
if aligned_end > start or block_size <= max_prefill_tokens:
    end = aligned_end
```

It does not know the overlay’s smaller mixed cap. At a block boundary, if the prompt needs more chunks and `mixed_cap < block_size <= max_prefill_tokens`, the capped chunk rounds down to zero. A waiting request then hits the alignment branch’s `break`; a running prefill takes the zero-token `continue`. Repeating this decision can reproduce starvation even with a positive mixed cap. This is an additional hazard when choosing a fix, not the explanation for the reporter’s `CHUNK=0` runs.

I executed the actual extracted alignment method with CPU request/configuration stubs. With MNBT 7168 and an illustrative 3584-token block:

| Global threshold | Proposed chunk | Aligned result at position zero of a 30k prompt |
|---:|---:|---:|
| Unset / `0` | 128 | **0** |
| 3584 | 128 | **0** |
| 1024 | 128 | 128 |
| Unset / `0` | 1024 | **0** |
| 1024 | 1024 | 1024 |

The same probes passed with block size 1792. This verifies the conditional code behavior, not the reporter’s actual block geometry. The repository has documented 3584-token hybrid geometry, but the live `cache_config.block_size` and cache mode should be recorded before relying on it. [Recorded hybrid geometry](DESIGN-apc-per-group-retention.md). In particular, do not recommend “mixed cap 128, global threshold 3584” as universally safe.

**The immediate mitigation is already measured, but incomplete.** For an operator who needs newcomers to make progress before a scheduler change, the evidence-backed starting configuration is:

```dotenv
GLM53_MIXED_PREFILL_CHUNK=0
LONG_PREFILL_TOKEN_THRESHOLD=1024
MAX_NUM_BATCHED_TOKENS=7168
MAX_NUM_SEQS=4
```

That is a degraded-service workaround: on the reported run A still fell to 2.8 tok/s during B’s prefill. The later `3584` default has useful evidence from other workloads, but no result here establishes that it protects A; even `1024` fails to do so adequately. Changing these two existing settings requires recreating/restarting the serving processes with the intended environment and arguments, not rebuilding the image. Verify the effective head environment and both rank commands, since exported launcher variables can override `.env`. The 2026-09-14 investigation did not restart. The 2026-09-15 enable used `CHUNK=fair` (not this `0`/`1024` workaround) and did restart.

A bounded tuning experiment could hold the global threshold at 1024 and sweep the positive mixed cap through 512, 256, and 128, **after confirming that alignment allows progress at that threshold and at every boundary**. Positive mixed caps apply only when a decoding peer exists, whereas the global threshold still caps solo prefill. Measure both A’s delivery gaps and B’s continuing progress. This sweep may find a tolerable compromise, but the existing 128-token long-history observation means success cannot be assumed. Changing MNBT globally is another throughput/latency experiment, not an independent fix for starvation.

**The durable fix is scheduling by service time as well as tokens.** An initial version was implemented on 2026-09-15 as opt-in `fair` in `overlay/patch_scheduler_decode_floor.py` and the scheduler it patches. v2 implements the shared gate, service aging, in-flight guard, and alignment cap; the follow-up source audit above identifies the missing contention accounting, adaptive cost control, and explicit decode reservation. Do not promote it to the multi-client default until the reporter-shaped overlap gates pass. Keep the legacy `skip` option available with its starvation behavior clearly documented. The following are the original design requirements, not a claim that v2 fully implements each one:

1. **Make one shared policy decision per engine step.** Apply it to both waiting prefills and partially completed running prefills. Track time since each request last received actual prefill service, so admission alone does not reset its entitlement. Reserve eligible decodes first, including speculative input/slot accounting, regardless of the order in which prefills entered `self.running`.

2. **Reserve periodic prefill service and regular decode-only steps.** Between prefill turns, run the existing uniform decode path. On a prefill turn, admit a bounded aggregate amount of prefill work, initially at most one prefill chunk, alongside eligible decodes if profiling favors mixed execution. Select prefills with aging and round-robin service so an admitted long prompt cannot monopolize those turns. A pure-prefill turn is an alternative to benchmark, but it pauses A for that turn too.

3. **Control elapsed GPU service, not just step counts.** Start with a conservative chunk cap and update a cost estimate from completed engine steps, accounting for prompt position/history and batch shape. Combine a configurable prefill time share with a maximum interval between prefill opportunities. A rule such as “one mixed step every N decodes” alone is inadequate when a mixed step takes seconds and a decode step takes milliseconds. Account for async work already in flight so stale timing cannot admit a burst of expensive chunks. Avoid introducing a GPU synchronization on every scheduler decision solely for measurement.

4. **Make the small-chunk policy compatible with hybrid alignment.** Pass the effective policy chunk limit into the alignment decision, or otherwise explicitly allow the base implementation’s private sub-block state progression under that limit. Preserve mandatory cache boundaries, partial-tail handling, and resumed-request replay. Do not remove alignment or publish an incomplete recurrent state as a reusable prefix entry. Distinguish an intentional policy cap from temporary residual capacity after other requests consume a step budget.

5. **Handle transitions and overload.** Classify work using the actual tokens still requiring computation, including resumed/recomputed output, cached prefixes, and async placeholders. Remove policy state on completion/abort. Yield a partial prefill without discarding its KV. Log separate deferral reasons for policy, alignment, token budget, sequence slots, and KV capacity. Fair service promises apply when a request can obtain a slot and memory; they cannot override resource exhaustion or make an overloaded queue have bounded latency.

This design bounds starvation by offering prefill turns while preserving useful stretches of fast decode. It necessarily spends some GPU time on B. The share devoted to prefill, B’s TTFT, and A’s throughput must be chosen together. A chunk already executing cannot be interrupted by a scheduler-side timer, so if the smallest safe chunk exceeds the delivery-gap target, scheduling alone cannot meet that target. That outcome calls for reducing the measured mixed-step costs or adding independently provisioned serving capacity. Splitting this two-rank model into a “prefill Spark” and a “decode Spark” is not a configuration-only solution; disaggregation would also need validated transfer of this model’s hybrid recurrent, sparse, and draft state.
